Can LLMs fully replace solvers?

In the reported benchmark, LLMs did not replace optimization solvers. The workflow used LLMs to propose structural hypotheses, computational backends to search and bound, and independent checkers to validate results.

Benchmark conclusion

What the benchmark establishes

The LLM did not independently certify feasibility, compute valid global dual bounds, or validate its own reductions. MILP solvers, SAT engines, exact dynamic programs, exhaustive searches, rational arithmetic, and deterministic original-model audits performed the computational search and verification tasks.

This is an empirical finding, not an impossibility theorem. The observed workflow did not replace solvers; the result does not establish that future systems cannot replace a particular computational component.

What “solve” meant

Roles in the computational workflow

LLM

Interpret and propose

Recover formulation semantics, connect matrix patterns to algorithms, propose reductions and invariants, design falsification tests, and coordinate certificate construction.

Backend

Search and bound

Find feasible points, optimize reduced models, enumerate finite state spaces, refute cutoffs, and compute numerical lower bounds or exact lower-bound certificates.

Checker

Accept or reject

Replay proof traces, verify rational certificates, audit every original row, test mapping directions, and prevent claims from exceeding their verified scope.

Acceptance rule: no benchmark result is accepted solely from an LLM output. A hypothesis is reported as a result only after computational testing and independent validation appropriate to the claim.

20-instance comparison

Primal and dual bounds favor different workflows

Choose a metric. “Skill-guided” means use of one structure-research workflow; it does not mean LLM-only.

The structure-guided arm produced the better primal bound on 11 of 20 instances, four strict primal-bound improvements over its frozen public baseline, and certified global optimality for two instances. The comparison arm produced the stronger dual bound on 12 of 20 and the smaller relative gap on 11. Improving primal bounds does not replace global dual bounding; strong branch-and-bound performance does not by itself yield a portable optimality certificate.

Six skill-arm primal bounds were tolerance-indexed. The two comparison-study certified global optima—ns1456591 and neos-3682128-sandon—belong to a separate comparison dataset and are not added to the main benchmark count of 30 resolved instances.

Requirements for a controlled benchmark

Design of a controlled solver-replacement study

A causal test should freeze identical MPS hashes, machines, solver versions, threads, seeds, starts, external information, tolerances, and wall-clock budgets. It should also reserve separate budgets for structural analysis, primal construction, dual search, proof production, and independent verification—then score unsupported claims and verification failures, not only final objective values.

Practical recommendation

Use the LLM to enlarge the hypothesis space and compress human research overhead. Use the best available computational backend for search and proof. Keep acceptance outside both.
